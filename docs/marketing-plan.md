# SimplyApply — Road to 100 students / 20 employers

Target date: **March 1, 2027** (before spring-break hiring on 30A).
Written Oct 22, 2026.

---

## 1. The ratio

**Target: 5 completed students : 1 employer location, inside a 15-mile radius.**

Your instinct of 100:20 is exactly 5:1, and it's right. Here's the derivation so you
can defend it and adjust it later:

- A teen part-time opening needs **4–6 applicants** to actually fill. No-show and
  ghosting rates at 14–18 are brutal.
- Each location opens roughly **2 roles per season** → ~10 applications needed.
- A motivated student applies to about **3 places** → 10 ÷ 3 ≈ **3.3 students** per location.
- Add a buffer for inactive and half-finished accounts → **5:1**.

**The two failure modes:**

| Ratio | What breaks |
|---|---|
| Below 3:1 | Employer opens the candidate list, sees 2 kids, decides the app is dead. Churns. |
| Above 8:1 | Students apply and get rejected or ignored. Churns, tells friends it doesn't work. |

5:1 is the middle of the safe band. Hold it as you grow — if employer signups
outpace students, stop selling and go recruit students, and vice versa.

### The part that actually matters

**The ratio has to hold inside one commute radius, not nationally.**

Right now you have students in Atlanta, San Antonio, Denver and Armagh. Those are
singletons with zero employers in range — they are unmatched and unmatchable. If
you hit 100 students spread across 30 states and 20 employers in Walton County,
your global ratio reads a perfect 5:1 and the app is still dead for 85 of those
students.

So the real goal is:

> **100 students and 20 employer locations, both in Walton County.**

Out-of-area signups are not progress. Park them (see §6) and don't count them.

## 3. Phase 0 — Fix the bucket (now → Nov 7)

Do not pour 88 students into a funnel that drops half of them.

1. **Close the onboarding leak.** 50% → 75% completion is worth 44 students, for
   free, with no marketing. Instrument where they drop, then fix that screen.
2. **Find out why zero applications have been sent.** This is the single most
   alarming number in the whole business. Either the apply button isn't
   discoverable, or there was nothing worth applying to. Sit down with two of your
   friends who signed up, watch them try to apply, say nothing, and take notes.
3. **Build the employer target list** (see below). Walking in cold with no list
   wastes the hardest-to-get resource you have: Saturdays.
4. **Confirm the live-mode `PRO25` coupon exists** and `EMAIL_SUPPRESS_LIST` is set
   in Vercel. Carried over from the build work — still unverified.

Deliverable by Nov 7: a named list of 120 businesses, and a funnel that doesn't leak.

## 4. Phase 1 — 20 employers (Nov 8 → Dec 20)

### The math

In-person walk-in conversion on a free product is roughly **15%**.
20 employers ÷ 0.15 ≈ **135 doors**. Budget for 150.

That's 6 Saturdays at 25 doors a day. Very doable.

### Where the doors are — in density order

1. **Silver Sands Premium Outlets** (Miramar Beach, Walton County) — ~110 stores in
   one walkable strip, nearly all of them part-time teen employers with chronic
   turnover. This is 20 employers in a single afternoon. **Start here.** Ask for the
   store manager, not whoever is on register.
2. **Grand Boulevard at Sandestin** — retail + dining cluster, same walk-in model.
3. **30A corridor** — Seaside, Rosemary Beach, Watercolor, Grayton, Blue Mountain.
   Restaurants, ice cream, surf shops, bike rentals, beach services. Highest
   seasonal teen demand in the county.
4. **Freeport / Santa Rosa Beach mainland** — Publix, fast food, car washes,
   landscaping. Less glamorous, hires year-round, less seasonal cliff.

### The highest-yield tactic: hunt hiring signs

Any business with a **"Now Hiring" / "Help Wanted"** sign in the window is
*pre-qualified*: demonstrated, current, urgent need. Walk the outlets and 30A with
your phone and photograph every single one. That list converts several times better
than cold doors, because you're not selling a concept — you're answering a problem
they already posted on their own glass.

### The pitch (30 seconds, lead with supply)

> "Hi — I'm Asher, I go to Seacoast. I built a free site that connects local
> students 14 to 21 with part-time jobs around here. I've got [N] students in the
> area right now looking for shifts. It's free to post — can I show you?"

Rules:
- **Lead with the number of students.** Concrete supply beats any product description.
- **Say "free" in the first two sentences.** It removes the whole defensive posture.
- **Never pitch Pro on the first visit.** You are buying inventory, not revenue.
- Don't leave without either a signup on the spot (best) or a manager's name,
  email, and a specific day you'll follow up.

Have your phone open to the employer onboarding screen. Sign them up standing
there. "I'll check it out later" converts at near zero.

### Supporting employer channels

- **Walton County Chamber of Commerce** — join or get a student membership. The
  member directory is your target list, and a chamber newsletter mention is free
  credibility with exactly the right audience.
- **Referrals from the 2–3 employers you already have.** Ask each for two names
  *before* you need them. A warm intro from another local owner converts ~3× a cold door.
- **Local business Facebook groups** — message the admin first, post from your
  personal profile, lead with the student count.

## 5. Phase 2 — 100 students (Jan 5 → Feb 28)

By January you have real jobs to show, which makes the student pitch trivially easy.
This is the right order: students are cheap to get and churn fast if there's nothing
to apply to.

### Channels, in order of yield per hour

1. **Group chats.** Nothing else on this list is close. Team chats, band, student
   council, youth group, class chats. One message from you — a student they know —
   into a 40-person chat beats any ad ever made. Ask friends to forward it to theirs.
   This alone can plausibly carry you to 60.
2. **Guidance counselors.** South Walton High, Seacoast Collegiate, Freeport High.
   You're a student there; that door is open to you and shut to competitors. Ask
   for: a line in the school newsletter, a mention to juniors/seniors, or a flyer
   on the counseling office door. Highest-trust channel available, and it comes
   with implicit parental approval.
3. **Parent Facebook groups.** Covered separately — parents are the decision-makers
   for 14–16 year olds, and you already have direct evidence (the girl whose *mom*
   told her about the app). Write for parents, not teens: "my kid wants a job and
   has no resume and no idea where to start." Message the admin first.
4. **Instagram / TikTok.** Where the teens actually are — Facebook is not. Local
   content, not product ads: "5 places in Santa Rosa Beach hiring 16-year-olds
   right now." That post is useful whether or not anyone signs up, which is why it
   gets shared. Post the jobs you now have.
5. **Physical QR codes** where teens stand around: the high school, Publix, the
   skate park, coffee shops. Cheap, low yield, non-zero.
6. **Keep the SEO.** It produced ~20 signups with zero effort and is the only
   channel that works while you sleep. Don't break it, and consider local landing
   pages ("part time jobs for 16 year olds in Santa Rosa Beach").

## 6. Phase 3 — Spring break (March 2027)

Beach-town hiring happens **February through April**, ahead of spring break and
summer. This is why the March 1 date exists: you want supply and demand both in
place *before* the wave, not during it.

In March, stop acquiring and start converting. The only metric that matters:

> **How many students actually got hired?**

One real hire changes everything. It's your first testimonial, your first case
study, the first thing you can say to an employer that isn't a projection, and the
first proof to yourself that the marketplace clears. Get one, write it up, and put
it on the landing page.

### Out-of-area signups

You'll keep collecting them from SEO. Don't delete and don't ignore — add a waitlist
state: "We're not in Denver yet. We'll email you the day we are." Costs you nothing,
builds a map of where to expand, and tells you which metro to open second.

  marketing. Stop everything and fix it.

## 8. What not to do

- **Don't go wide.** 100 students across 20 cities is worth less than 30 in one town.
  Geographic density is the entire product.
- **Don't pay for ads yet.** You have no idea what your conversion or retention is.
  Ads on an unproven funnel just buy you noise faster.
- **Don't sell Pro yet.** 20 employers who've never received an application won't
  pay $20/month, and asking makes the free pitch harder. Revenue comes after the
  first hires.
- **Don't scale past 100/20 until one student has been hired.** Growth before
  product-market fit just makes the churn bigger.
